The certification path

How to become a USATH referee

Four levels, one committee behind you. Most people reach Level 1 in one season while still playing.

Who can start

Players, former players, coaches, parents, club volunteers. No refereeing experience needed: playing helps you read the game, the rules you learn here. Officiated another sport? Say so, the NRC can shorten the path.

The steps

  1. Tell the NRC you are interested

    Use the contact button on this page. A member of the committee picks up the conversation, answers your questions and, when you are ready, invites you to the hub so you can set up your profile.

  2. Complete the prerequisites

    SafeSport certification, a background screening and a USATH referee membership on the Sport80 platform. These are required before any certification is issued and must stay active every season.

  3. Start learning the rules now

    Use the training module: flashcards and quizzes built from the IHF Rules of the Game and the IHF Catalogue of Rules Questions, sorted by rule and by level. Ten minutes a day is enough to be ready for the theory test.

  4. Level 1 course and test, on this platform

    Once invited, you follow the Level 1 online course in the training module: six modules built on the IHF rules and the IHF Catalogue of Rules Questions, each with a study pass and a short module test. The platform tracks your progress, lets you schedule your study sessions with calendar reminders, and unlocks the final theory test when every module is complete. Pass the test and Level 1 is recorded on your profile automatically. The USATH entry course also covers whistle technique, hand signals and a physical test (shuttle run by age group); the NRC schedules those at clinics.

  5. Whistle games and get observed

    The NRC nominates Level 1 referees to local and regional tournaments, usually in a pair with a more experienced partner. Observers give feedback after games, and that feedback is recorded on your profile here so you and the committee can track progress.

  6. Level 2 and Level 3 (National)

    Promotion comes from performance at tournaments, observer evaluations and the theory and physical tests. Level 3 referees are nominated to USATH sanctioned events, national championships and collegiate championships.

  7. Continental and international

    The NRC recommends its strongest pairs to the NACHC Playing Rules and Referees Commission for continental events, and from there to the IHF Global Referee Training Programme.

From first contact to your first game

1. Contact

You fill in the short form on this page. The NRC answers within a few days.

2. Invitation and profile

The NRC adds you to the roster as a candidate and sends you a sign in link. You complete your profile: club, home city, closest airport, availability.

3. Level 1 course

Six online modules, about three weeks at two sessions a week. Study cards, then a module test. Progress is saved on your profile.

4. Prerequisites

SafeSport, background screening and the USATH referee membership on Sport80, tracked on your profile with expiry dates.

5. Final test

Timed theory test drawn from the question bank. Reach the pass mark and Level 1 is recorded on your profile with the date.

6. First game

The NRC nominates you to a local event, paired with an experienced referee, and an observer gives you feedback after your games.

What your club gets

A club with its own certified pair can host sanctioned games, run youth leagues and send players to tournaments without depending on referees from other regions. The NRC's One Certified Pair Per Club program pairs two club members with a mentor referee and a training plan. Ask your club board to nominate you.
