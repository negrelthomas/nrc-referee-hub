Admin guide

What each part of the hub does, what it is for, and the order things happen in. Written for the NRC administrators.

1. Dashboard

The first screen after sign in. It shows the recurring tasks the committee owes (the Sport80 import every 30 days turns red when overdue and only clears when the import runs), the size and certification of the roster, club coverage against the one certified pair per club goal, new contact requests, referees who have not taken their baseline assessment, programs in progress and overdue, the weakest rules across the pool (a direct input for clinic content) and the compliance watch (expired or missing membership, SafeSport, background check).

2. Referees

The roster. One record per referee: identity, club, home city and airports, certification level and date, Sport80 member ID, compliance dates, availability notes and admin notes. Open a referee to see and edit everything, add a certification, log a game sheet or record an evaluation. Add referee creates a record by hand; the record's email is what allows the person to sign in (sign in is by invitation only: an email that is not on the roster or in the admin list gets no link). Export CSV gives the roster as a spreadsheet. To update many records at once, use Imports.

Removing someone: set the record inactive rather than deleting it, so tournament history and evaluations stay attached.

3. Inquiries

People who pressed "I want to be contacted" on the public Become a referee page. Each row carries their answers: where they are, club if any, background, availability, message. Work them in order: contact the person, then "Add to roster" when they are ready (it opens a prefilled candidate record; save it and they can sign in), set the status to invited. The dashboard counts the new ones.

4. Clubs

The USATH club list with city, state and nearest airport, used for coverage statistics and for travel planning. Each club has a pair program status (not started, nominated, mentor assigned, in training, observed, certified pair) that you update as the club progresses. Correct airports and cities here; they drive the "local" flag when staffing.

5. Staffing, tournament requests and travel

Organizers submit a request on the public page: dates, venue, pairs needed, whether a supervisor is needed, who books travel (the organizer, or the NRC with the organizer reimbursing), and what they cover (hotel nights, flights, transfers, food, earliest departure). The request arrives with status submitted. The organizer can sign in with the email of the request and follow it on their own page: progress, designated officials with contact details, and travel bookings when the NRC books. The Staffing page shows one card per event to staff with progress; a card opens the full dashboard. The intended flow:

  1. Review. Open the request, set the sanction and region, set status to reviewing. Note the supervisor need in the header.
  2. Find referees. The Find referees tab lists certified referees, best first: those who declared themselves available for the tournament weekends, then tentative, then not declared; unavailable ones are hidden by default. Local referees (same airport as the venue) are flagged, as are established pairs and season load. Tick the referees to propose and add them as draft invitations. Propose both members of a pair when you want the pair.
  3. Send invitations. Select the drafts and send. Each referee gets an email with the event, what is covered, and three answers: accept, tentative, decline, plus a prompt to sign in. The same proposal appears on their referee home. Answers show on the Invitations tab as they come in; send a reminder after a few days to those still waiting.
  4. Confirm. Select the accepted referees and confirm. The hub checks that the confirmed count matches the pairs requested and that established pairs stay together, and warns if not. Confirming emails each referee the full brief (dates, venue, airport, earliest departure, partner, the rest of the team, organizer contact, coverage) and records the assignment; the tournament moves to staffed.
  5. Notify organizer. Sends the organizer the designated list and the link to their private tournament page, where they see names, levels, clubs, contact details and travel notes, and the coverage they committed to. Send it again after any change.
  6. Designated and travel. This tab lists who is designated with their contact and home airport, shows who else is open to officiate (accepted or tentative but not designated), lets you add a supervisor or observer directly, and remove a designated referee (their invitation is marked declined; pick a replacement from Find referees). When the NRC books travel, record each flight, hotel, car or train with provider, reference, times, cost and who pays; the referee sees every booking on their home with a calendar file, and the organizer sees them on their page. When the organizer books, record what they tell you so the referee still has one place to look.
  7. Changes and cancellations. Organizers can edit their request (dates, venue, pairs, coverage, who books travel) or cancel it from their page; every change is logged and emailed to the NRC and to the designated officials. The NRC edits the main fields in the tournament header and uses Notify changes to send the same email; a cancelled event sends a cancellation notice. Send brief sends each designated official a role specific confirmation (referee, supervisor, observer) with the full details.
  8. After the event. The organizer files a two minute report from their page (games played, games per official, ratings, incidents); it fills the Officiated and games tab and shows under Expenses. You can still record or correct games on that tab. Referees submit expense claims with receipts from their home; the Expenses tab shows the money picture (bookings paid by the NRC or the organizer, claims to review), lets you approve, reject or mark reimbursed, and sets who reimburses each claim. Organizers see the totals on their page.
  9. Automatic reminders, every morning at 8am Pacific. Unanswered invitations after 7 days (and once more at 14), designated officials without a booked flight or hotel within 21 days when the NRC books, organizers without a report 3 days after the event, overdue training programs. Each person is reminded at most once per item; you receive a digest at referee@handballusa.org of everything the job did and saw.

Emails go out from the NRC Referee Hub sender configured on Netlify and are logged; a send that fails is shown with the reason. Referees contact organizers only once confirmed; organizers reach referees through their tournament page.

6. Questions

The question bank behind the training module, the Level 1 course, the assessments and the programs: the IHF Catalogue of Rules Questions (2025 edition) plus the game situation deck. Each question has a rule number, a difficulty (Level 1 to 3), options and the correct answer with the rule reference. Edit, deactivate or add questions here; difficulty decides which pool a referee is tested on.

7. Training sessions

Clinics, online rule sessions and certification courses the NRC announces. Published sessions appear on the public Training page under Next training sessions, with date, time zone, location, audience and registration link.

8. Programs

Two tracks run automatically on the training page, under My program:

Candidates (level candidate) follow the Level 1 online course: six modules by rule group, each with study cards and a module test, a study plan they can push to their calendar, then a timed final test. A pass records "theory passed" with the date on their profile (visible in the Referees list); the NRC schedules the practical part (signals, whistle, physical test) at a clinic and then sets the level to Level 1 on the record. Referees whose level was entered from correspondence carry an "NRC to confirm" badge until you tick "Level confirmed" on their record.

Certified referees take a baseline assessment at first sign in: 50 questions at their level by default. At or above the hurdle, they are cleared. Below, the hub builds a program on every rule they scored under the threshold, due in 12 weeks, with study sessions exported to their calendar and a module test per rule, then a re-test; a re-test below the hurdle builds a new program. The Programs page shows every referee's state (signed in or not, baseline date, last score, program status, modules done, due date) and lets you change the parameters of both tracks.

9. Imports

Roster CSV: create or update many referees at once from a spreadsheet (match on email, then on name; empty cells keep the current value). Use it for the initial roster and after each NRC review. Sport80 export: refreshes membership IDs and expiry dates; this is the recurring 30 day task. IHF catalogue: loads a new edition of the question catalogue from its text.

10. What referees see

After sign in, a referee home with: next assignment, proposals waiting for an answer, compliance alerts, the weekends they declare available, tentative or unavailable (this is what the staffing screen reads), blocked dates, training nudges. Tabs for profile (editable by the referee, level set by the NRC), tournaments officiated, game log, evaluations shared with them, assignments, training results by rule and level. The public Referees page lists certified referees with level, certified since, last certification and club, no contact details.

11. Accounts and transfer

Hosting (Netlify), database and sign in (Supabase), code (GitHub) and the sending mailbox are held by Thomas Negrel until the hub is transferred to USATH; the transfer checklist is in the README of the code repository. Administrators are added by inserting their email in the admin list before their first sign in.

Imports

Referee roster (CSV)

Import or update the roster from a spreadsheet saved as CSV (Excel: File > Save as > CSV). Matched on email, otherwise on first and last name; new emails create a candidate record. Columns recognized, any order: first name, last name, email, phone, club (name as in the Clubs list), city, state, airport, level (candidate, 1, 2, 3, nachc, ihf), certified or cert date, member id, membership expiry, safesport, background, notes. Empty cells leave the current value in place.

Sport80 membership export

Export the referee membership list from Sport80 as CSV. The importer matches on email (then on first and last name) and updates the membership validity date and member ID. Expected columns, any order, header names flexible: email, first name, last name, member id, expiry or valid until. Unmatched rows are listed for review and nothing is created automatically.

IHF Catalogue of Rules Questions

Paste the text of the IHF catalogue (questions file) and, separately, the answers file. The parser recognizes the numbering 7.12) with options a) to d), assigns the rule number from the question number, and marks the correct options from the answers file. Review in the Questions tab afterwards; everything imports as Level 2 by default.
