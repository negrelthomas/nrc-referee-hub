Evaluators

Who can evaluate, how to get certified, how to rate a referee pair on the IHF sheet and write a report the NRC can release.

1. Who can evaluate

  • Referees, club members, coaches and NRC members. Club affiliation is optional.
  • Everyone passes the same two tests. Tournament organizers evaluate only once certified.
  • Join from the evaluator page (signed in referees and organizers), or ask the NRC to add you.
  • Never evaluate a game you refereed (the hub blocks it). Declare games of your own club and family links to the NRC.

2. Certification

  1. Rules exam. 40 questions from the IHF Catalogue of Rules Questions across all 18 rules, 45 minutes, 80% to pass. Renewed every 12 months.
  2. Video calibration. Rate three NRC reference games on video with the IHF sheet. A rating within one step of the NRC reference counts as agreement; the summary verdict must match. You pass at 80% average over your first attempts. After each game you see the reference rating and remarks. Practice attempts are allowed, but only the first attempt on a game counts. Renewed every 24 months.
  3. Certified. Games to evaluate open. Your first two released reports are read in full by the NRC (probation).

Defaults set by the NRC in Admin > Evaluators. The structure follows public federation practice: EHF delegate candidates take rules tests and evaluate a recorded game; FFHandball supervisors pass a rules assessment and supervised observations, with periodic renewal.

3. The rating scale

RatingDefinition
-- Not satisfactoryMany mistakes, loss of control, influencing the game
- PoorMany mistakes, no influence, little control
0 AverageSeveral mistakes, but regular control
+ GoodFew mistakes and good control
++ Very goodAlmost no mistakes, very good control
+++ ExcellentPractically perfect
  • Decide each item on the definition: number of mistakes and quality of match control. When unsure between two steps, reread the definition.
  • An item that did not occur is rated Average (for example Shootout when there was none).
  • 21 items, from Fair play and neutrality to Cooperation with the table. Tap an item name in the form to read its definition.
  • Summary: Positive, Neutral or Negative. Type of match: Easy to Very difficult. Influence: reducing difficulties, no influence, increasing difficulties.

4. Writing a report

  1. Open Games to evaluate. The default list shows games with a video link that nobody has evaluated yet.
  2. Press Evaluate. The video plays above the form on a laptop; on a phone, open it in the YouTube app and come back.
  3. Record the match facts (half scores, 7 m, cards) if you have them.
  4. Rate the 21 items, then the summary, type of match and influence.
  5. Remarks: the NRC must be able to understand your ratings from the text.
    • Positive remarks: what the pair did well, with the situations that show it.
    • Negative remarks: errors and their cause. Key scenes, not every mistake minute by minute.
    • Suggestions: two or three concrete points for the next game.
    • Key scenes: the video timestamp, the item and what the call should have been.
  6. Add an individual note per referee when one of them needs it.
  7. Save draft at any time (the form also keeps your work on the device), then Submit to the NRC within three days of the game.

5. NRC review

  • Every report goes to the NRC first. The NRC releases it to both referees or returns it with a note.
  • A returned report reopens for editing under My reports; submit it again once corrected.
  • Released reports appear on each referee's Evaluations tab with the full sheet, and count in the NRC rankings.
  • Ranking score: summary verdict (Positive 4, Neutral 3, Negative 2), plus or minus 1 for the referees' influence, weighted by type of match (Easy 0.75 to Very difficult 1.5). Proposal for the NRC to confirm.

6. For the NRC

  1. Admin > Referees > Evaluators. Reports to review, evaluators with their exam and calibration results, the calibration library and the certification rules.
  2. Founding evaluator. The NRC chair is certified directly and sets the reference ratings. Other founding evaluators can be ticked in the evaluator record.
  3. Calibration library. Add a reference game (title, video link, what to watch), then rate it. Or open a released report with a video and use it as a reference. Keep five to eight games of different levels so a candidate who fails can attempt new ones.
  4. Add an evaluator. Name, email, club or no club affiliation, background. They sign in with that email and land on the evaluator page.
  5. Video. Games need a video link in the game log to be reviewed remotely. Ask organizers and clubs to add stream links when they log games.
