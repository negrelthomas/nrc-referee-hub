Referees

How to sign in, declare your availability, answer invitations, follow your trip, submit expenses and train.

1. Sign in

  1. Open the sign in page and type the email address the NRC has for you. No password exists.
  2. Open the email "Your sign in link" and click Sign in. You land on your referee home.
  3. If the link says it is no longer valid (some mail apps open links by themselves), go back to the sign in page and type the code from the same email.
  4. Not on the roster yet? The page says so. Write to the NRC; they add you and you can sign in with that email.

2. Your home

The top row shows your level, tournaments officiated, games this season and evaluations shared with you. Below it, in order: your next confirmed assignment with the trip card, the alerts that need you (an invitation to answer, a program to take, a document the NRC does not have on file, a missing photo), your weekend availability, and a training nudge.

  1. Home is the summary. Profile is your details and photo. Tournaments and Games are your history, fed by the organizers' reports. Evaluations shows what observers shared with you. Assignments lists every invitation and its status. Training is your score by rule and your program status.
  2. On Profile, add a photo, your phone, club, home city and your home airport: the NRC uses the airport to plan travel and to find local referees for an event. Press Save profile. Your certification level is set by the NRC and cannot be edited here.

3. Availability

  1. On Home, the Weekends I can referee grid shows the next 26 weekends. Tap a weekend to cycle it: available (green), tentative (amber), unavailable (red). It saves on each tap.
  2. Weekends you leave blank count as unknown; the NRC sees unknown as "ask first", so declaring your weekends gets you considered first.
  3. For longer periods (club season, travel), use Blocked dates: from, to, optional note, then Block dates. Remove a block with the Remove button.

4. Answer an invitation

When the NRC proposes you for an event you get an email with the event details, what the organizer covers (flights, hotel nights, transfers, food) and three buttons. The same proposal sits on your home with the same buttons.

  1. Click Accept, Tentative or Decline in the email, or on your home. The page that opens lets you add a note for the NRC (flight constraints, partner preference), then press Send answer. Nothing is recorded before you press it.
  2. Tentative means the NRC sees you as possible but cannot designate you. When you know, open your home or the Assignments tab and press Confirm now or decline.
  3. The NRC confirms the final pairs. You then get a "Confirmed" email with the full brief: dates, venue, partner, team, organizer contact, coverage and how travel is booked. The assignment appears at the top of your home.
  4. If the team gets completed without you, you receive a short email saying your invitation is closed. Your availability stays on file.
  5. No answer after 7 days: the hub sends one reminder, then a second one a week later.

5. Your trip, bookings and expenses

  1. Once confirmed, the trip card on your home shows the venue, nearest airport, organizer contact and who books travel. When the NRC books, every flight, hotel and car appears here as it is booked: provider, reference, times, cost. When the organizer books, the organizer deals with you directly and the NRC records what they confirm.
  2. Add to my calendar downloads a calendar file with the tournament and each booking (flight times, hotel check in and check out, car pickup). Open it with Google Calendar, Outlook or Apple Calendar.
  3. Booked something yourself? Add a booking I made records it so the NRC and the organizer see the whole picture, with the cost to reimburse.
  4. Submit an expense: type, amount, description and a photo of the receipt. The NRC approves or rejects it and marks it reimbursed; the status shows under the trip card.
  5. I need to withdraw opens an email to the NRC. Do not simply not show up: a withdrawal received early lets the NRC find a replacement.

6. Training

The Training page works without signing in, but signed in your results are saved on your profile and the NRC sees your score by rule.

  1. Practice quiz: pick a level, a rule and a source, choose 10 to 25 questions, press Start. After each answer the explanation shows the correct answer and the rule reference. Optional: tick Clock per question and set 10 to 30 seconds; the clock stops while you read the explanation.
  2. Timed challenge: a clock on every question, the next one comes by itself. The format used on the court.
  3. Flashcards: tap to reveal, mark it known or missed, review the missed ones.
  4. My progress: sessions and score by rule. Rules below 70% are highlighted; a Practice button opens a quiz on that rule.

7. Candidates: the Level 1 online course

  1. On Home, press Open on the Level 1 online course alert, or open Training and the My program tab.
  2. Six modules by rule group. For each: Study opens the cards, then Done studying, take the module test. The test is 10 questions, 70% to pass; you see what you missed and can retake it.
  3. Study plan: pick a start date and press Save plan and download calendar. The hub lays out two 45 minute sessions a week and gives you a calendar file with a reminder for each.
  4. When all six modules are completed the final test unlocks: 30 questions across every rule group, 30 minutes, 80% to pass, no explanations until the end.
  5. Pass it and Level 1 theory passed is recorded on your profile with the date. The NRC contacts you for the practical part (signals, whistle, physical test) and awards Level 1 after it.

8. Certified referees: assessment and program

  1. At your first sign in, Home asks you to take the baseline assessment: 50 questions at the level of your certification, spread across every rule group, 60 minutes, no explanations until the end.
  2. The hurdle is 80% overall with every rule at 80% or more. Clear it and nothing further is required this season.
  3. Otherwise the hub builds your program from the rules you missed, weakest first, due in 12 weeks. Each rule is a module: study the cards, pass a 10 question test at 80%.
  4. Press Save plan and download calendar to put two study sessions a week in your calendar until the deadline.
  5. When every module is done the re-test opens. Clear the hurdle and the program closes; fall short and a new program is built from that result. Overdue programs get a reminder email and show on the NRC dashboard.
