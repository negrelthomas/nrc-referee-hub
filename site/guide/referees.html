Quick learns

Short videos of each action, click by click. 10 to 25 seconds each, no sound. The people and events shown are examples.

Sign in

Get in

  • Open the sign in page and type the email address the NRC has for you. There is no password.
  • You receive an email with a link and a code. Click the link, or type the code on the sign in page.
  • If the link says it is no longer valid (some mail apps open links by themselves), type the code instead. Only the newest email works.
  • Not on the roster yet: the page says so. Write to the NRC at referee@handballusa.org.

First sign in

  • A five screen welcome tour asks for what USATH does not have: phone, club, home city, airports and a photo.
  • Press Skip for now to do it later. Replay it from Welcome tour at the top of your home.

Your home

  • Top row: your level, tournaments officiated, games this season, evaluations shared with you.
  • Then, in order: your next confirmed tournament and trip, Your profile (how complete it is, with what is left to do), the alerts that need you (an invitation, a document about to expire), your weekends, and a training suggestion.
  • Your profile counts what you fill in (photo, phone, home, airport, member ID, weekends, assessment or Level 1 course) and what the NRC records (physical test, membership, SafeSport, background check). Each open item has a button that takes you there. The ? next to Physical test shows what pass means: the beep test level for your age and gender.
  • Tabs: Profile (your details), Tournaments and Games (your history), Evaluations, Assignments (every invitation and its status), Training (your score by rule).

Answer an invitation

  • When the NRC proposes you for an event, you get an email with the dates, venue and what the organizer covers. The same invitation sits on your home.
  • Press Accept, Tentative or Decline on your home or in the email.
  • Tentative keeps you in the list, but the NRC cannot designate you until you accept. Accept or decline from the Assignments tab when you know.
  • Accepting is not yet a designation. The NRC designates the final pairs; the weekends of the event are then marked as booked on your calendar, and the designation email has a link to decline if you cannot officiate after all (the NRC is told at once). Without an answer, the designation stands. You then receive the full brief by email, and the event moves to the top of your home.
  • No answer after 7 days: one reminder, then a second a week later.

Declare when you can referee

  • When I can referee shows the next 26 weekends. Tap a weekend to cycle it: available (green), tentative (amber), unavailable (red). Each tap saves. A weekend covers Friday to Sunday.
  • Some competitions run during the week. Events during the week lists the upcoming events with game days from Monday to Thursday: press Available, Tentative or Unavailable for each. Your answer covers all the event dates; press the same answer again to clear it.
  • For any other period, weekdays included, use Other dates: from, to, available, tentative or unavailable, a note if useful, then Save these dates. Remove deletes a period. Unavailable dates win over everything else.
  • Dates left blank count as unknown. For an event, the NRC sees you as available only when every day of the event is declared available; when some days are missing, it sees which ones.
  • Mark unavailable only the dates you cannot referee at all. Tournaments you are designated for already show as Assigned on their weekend: do not mark those dates unavailable, or the NRC reads that you can no longer officiate there. The hub warns you if unavailable dates cover one of them.
  • If the NRC needs you on dates you declared unavailable, an admin can mark you available for that one tournament. You get an email with the reason and answer Accept the change or Revert; your home shows it until you answer. Your other dates are not touched.

Your trip, bookings and expenses

  • Once you are designated, the trip card shows the venue, nearest airport, organizer contact and travel notes.
  • When the NRC books travel, each flight, hotel and car appears with its reference and times. When the organizer books, the NRC records what the organizer confirms.
  • Add to my calendar downloads one file with the tournament and every booking, for Google Calendar, Outlook or Apple Calendar.
  • Add a booking I made records travel you booked yourself, with its cost.
  • Expenses (card below the trip, for upcoming events and those of the last four months): press Submit an expense and attach the receipt, a photo or a PDF. The hub reads it and fills in the category, merchant, date, total and currency; check each field, then submit. One claim per receipt.
  • Categories: Airfare, Hotel (with the number of nights), Rideshare and taxis, Car rental and related (fuel, parking, tolls). Meals are covered by the food per diem, which the organizer adds; no receipt for it.
  • Paid in another currency: enter the amount and currency as on the receipt. The hub converts it to USD at the average ECB reference rate over the 30 days ending on the date of purchase, not your card's rate.
  • The organizer of the event approves or declines each claim and pays it. The status shows under each claim (waiting for the organizer, approved, declined with the reason, paid), and you get one email a day when something changes. Each claim also shows whether it falls within what the organizer agreed on the request. You can withdraw a claim until the organizer decides.
  • How I get paid: your Venmo username, PayPal or Zelle email or phone. Only the organizer who owes you sees it. No bank account or card numbers on the hub; agree a bank transfer directly with the organizer.
  • I need to withdraw writes to the NRC. Tell them early so they can find a replacement.

Profile, photo and email

  • On Profile, add a square photo, your phone, club, home city and state, and your airports. The NRC uses the airports to plan travel and to find local referees.
  • Press Save profile at the bottom.
  • Your level and your compliance dates (membership, SafeSport, background check) are set by the NRC and shown for information.
  • Your email is your sign in. To change it, press Change email under it and type the new address. A confirmation link goes to your current and your new inbox; the change applies once both are opened. Until then, keep signing in with the current address.

History and evaluations

  • Tournaments lists every event you refereed, with its divisions (for example Senior Men, U18 Women) and your games. Click an event for its dates, venue and the games you refereed. Counts at the top by sanction: USATH, regional, club, international.
  • Games lists every game logged with you in the pair: date, teams, division, partner, score, video link, and your evaluation score once the NRC shares it.
  • Evaluations opens on your latest three evaluations: physical test, theory (in person exam or online assessment, whichever is newer) and in game. Below are your tests and the observer reports the NRC shared with you.
  • On the public Referees page, your card shows your photo, club and tournaments with games per year. Your level is visible to you and the NRC only. Visitors never see evaluations or contact details.
  • To let other referees reach you, tick Show my email and phone to other signed in referees on your profile. They then appear in the referee directory, for signed in referees only.

Training

  • Sign in first: your results are saved on your profile and the NRC sees your score by rule.
  • Quiz: pick a level, a rule and a source, then Practice (no clock, explanation and rule reference after each answer) or Timed challenge (a clock on each question, the next one comes by itself).
  • Leaderboard: timed sessions of 10 questions or more earn points, one point per correct answer, per season. You appear only if you tick Show me on the timed challenge leaderboard on your profile; you always see your own row. Levels are not shown.
  • Flashcards: each card shows the question and its answer options; pick yours, tap to reveal the correct answer and the rule, mark known or missed, review the missed ones.
  • My progress: your score by rule. Rules below 70% are highlighted, with a button to practice them.

Candidates: the Level 1 online course

  • On your home, press Open on the Level 1 course alert, or open Training and My program.
  • Six modules by rule group. For each: Study, then the module test (10 questions, 70% to pass, retake as needed).
  • Save plan and download calendar puts two 45 minute sessions a week in your calendar.
  • When the six modules are done, the final test opens: 30 questions, 30 minutes, 80% to pass.
  • Pass it and "Level 1 theory passed" is recorded on your profile. The NRC contacts you for the practical part and awards Level 1 after it.

Certified referees: assessment and program

  • At your first sign in, your home asks for the initial (baseline) assessment: 50 questions at your level across every rule group, 60 minutes. Take it within 7 days of your first sign in: it gives the NRC a first picture of where every referee stands on the rules. Until it is done, a reminder email with a direct link to the test goes out every two days.
  • The hurdle is 80% overall and 80% on every rule. Clear it and nothing more is needed this season.
  • Otherwise the hub builds your program from the rules you missed, due in 12 weeks. Each rule is a module: study, then a 10 question test at 80%.
  • When every module is done, the re-test opens. Clear the hurdle and the program closes.

Referees from outside USATH

  • A pair from outside USATH is named on an event by the organizer or the NRC. Each referee with an email gets a sign in link to their own page.
  • The page shows the events they are named for: dates, venue, organizer contact, the officials on the event, what the organizer covers, their bookings and expenses, a calendar file.
  • At first sign in they are asked for a few details (photo, phone, home, airport, level in their federation, how they are reimbursed). They can skip and complete them later; the NRC or the organizer can also fill them in.
  • Training, the rules assessment and the program are open to them like any USATH referee, at the level of their federation. All optional: no deadline, no reminder, no evaluation, no ranking.

Common questions

The sign in link does not work

  • Type the code from the same email on the sign in page. Only the newest email counts if you asked more than once.

My level is wrong

  • The NRC sets it. Write to referee@handballusa.org with your certification date. An "NRC to confirm" badge means the level came from correspondence and is being checked.

Who sees my evaluations?

  • You and the NRC. Visitors to the Referees page see your photo, club, home city and tournaments, nothing else. Your level stays between you and the NRC. Other signed in referees see your email and phone only if you opt in on your profile.
  • Every score (assessment, re-test, Level 1 test, quizzes, learning path) is visible to you and the NRC only. The one exception is the timed challenge leaderboard, and only if you opt in.

I accepted but can no longer go

  • Press I need to withdraw on the trip card, or write to the NRC as early as possible.

Something does not work or could be better

  • Press the yellow Give feedback button at the bottom right of any page. It goes straight to the people building the hub.
