Table officials: timekeeper and scorekeeper

What the timekeeper and the scorekeeper do before, during and after a game, with the IHF rules behind each task. The technical delegate has a separate guide.

This module is in development with the NRC. The duties below follow the IHF Rules of the Game (Rule 2, Rule 4, Rule 16, Rule 18, the Clarifications and the Substitution Area Regulations). Certification for table officials is not defined yet. Practice quizzes: Training, Table officials. The technical delegate is a separate role with its own guide and training track. 1. Who does what

  • Timekeeper. Main responsibility for the playing time, team time-outs and the running time of suspensions (Rule 18:1).
  • Scorekeeper. Main responsibility for the team lists, the score sheet, players who arrive after the start and players not entitled to participate (Rule 18:1).
  • Both. Control the substitution area with the referees and assist each other (Rule 18:1, Substitution Area Regulations).
  • Technical delegate, when there is one. Not a table official: appointed for some events to supervise the table and the substitution areas (Clarification 7). See the technical delegate guide.

2. Before the game

  • Check the clock, the horn or whistle and the scoreboard. Agree a back up for time if there is no public clock (Rule 2:3).
  • Collect both team lists: up to 16 players and 4 team officials, with the responsible team official named (Rule 4).
  • Check the substitution areas are marked and the benches clear (Substitution Area Regulations).
  • Have the green time-out cards (three per team), suspension cards and the score sheet ready.
  • Brief with the referees: signals, how the table will alert them, how suspension ends are communicated.

3. During the game

  • Clock. Start on the referees' whistle for the throw off; stop only on the referees' time-out signal or a team time-out (Rule 2:8, 2:10).
  • Team time-out. Granted to the team in possession when it places the green card on the table. Stop the clock and signal with the whistle. Three per team in regular time, at most two per half, one in the last five minutes (Rule 2:10).
  • Suspensions. Record the player number and the time, run the two minutes on playing time, and tell the team when the player may re-enter (Rule 16).
  • Substitution area. Watch for faulty substitutions and illegal entries; alert the referees as the regulations require.
  • Final signal. End each half with the final signal if there is no automatic signal (Rule 2:3).

4. Interrupting the game

  • The timekeeper or the technical delegate interrupts at once for a faulty substitution or an illegal entry (Rule 18:1, Clarification 7).
  • The referees take the decision; the table explains what it saw. The restart depends on the reason and on who had the ball: see Clarification 7.
  • For a violation the referees did not notice, the table informs them at the next interruption.

5. After the game

  • Complete the score sheet, get it checked and signed as the event requires.
  • Report any disqualification with a written report and any incident at the table.
  • Hand the sheet to the organizer; the score on it is what the hub takes as the official result.

6. The score sheet

The NRC uses the score sheet to check game logs and, in AI assisted reviews, as the reference for the score. Record:

  • Goals by player and the running score.
  • Every warning, suspension and disqualification, with the minute and the player or official.
  • Every 7 metre throw, scored or not.
  • Every team time-out, with the minute.

7. Working with the technical delegate

  • When a technical delegate is appointed, the table works under the delegate's supervision. The timekeeper and scorekeeper keep their own duties.
  • Agree before the game who interrupts for a faulty substitution: the timekeeper or the delegate (Clarification 7).
  • Give the delegate the score sheet to check before it is signed.
